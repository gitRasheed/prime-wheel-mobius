1. THE STRONGEST REFUTATION

The exponential-sum inequality is mathematically correct as an absolute-constant completion bound, but QSCL as declared is false because its companion multiplicity claim is false and does not actually bound frequency-pair collisions.

For
S = Σ_{r<H} e((b/q)(N+r+1)²),  gcd(b,q)=1,
put g = gcd(2b,q). Complete quadratic sums satisfy
|G(b,h;q)| ≤ √(gq).
Periodicity modulo q, division into complete periods, and finite Fourier completion of the remainder give
|S| ≪ √g(H/√q + √q log(2q)).
Since b/q is reduced, g=gcd(2,q)≤2. Thus the declaration’s
H/√q + √q·log q
is valid after absorbing at most √2 into the implicit constant. For an explicit-constant Lean theorem, the clean uniform form should retain √g or split odd and even q.

Edge cases:

- If q is odd, g=1.
- If q is even, g=2. The shifted complete Gauss sum may vanish or have magnitude √(2q), depending on the parity of its linear coefficient. Completing the square by inverting 2 is invalid here, but the uniform √(2q) bound remains valid.
- If H>q, complete periods produce the H/√q term.
- If H≤q, the useful statement is
  |S| ≪ min{H, √g·√q log(2q)}.
  At H=1, completion is entirely vacuous: |S|=1 exactly, while the proposed upper bound is much larger.
- If b/q were not reduced, a factor involving gcd(2b,q) would be essential rather than an absolute constant.

The relevant standard method is finite Fourier completion plus complete quadratic Gauss-sum evaluation. Iwaniec–Kowalski place Gauss sums in §3.4 and “Completing methods” in §12.2; their Chapter 8 is Weyl/van der Corput rather than the book’s completion section. See [Iwaniec–Kowalski, Analytic Number Theory](https://unina2.on-line.it/sebina/repository/catalogazione/documenti/Iwaniec%2C%20Kowalski%20-%20Analytic%20Number%20Theory.pdf). Explicit square-root estimates for incomplete Gaussian sums are also treated by [Korolev, “On incomplete Gaussian sums”](https://m.mathnet.ru/eng/tm3644).

The multiplicity assertion is wrong. The declaration freezes
“≤ 2^ω(q) via root-counting of (n+1)² ≡ w (mod q)”
without requiring q squarefree, q odd, or gcd(w,q)=1. Two immediate counterexamples are:

- q=9, w=0: x²≡0 mod 9 has x=0,3,6, hence three roots, but 2^ω(9)=2.
- q=8, w=1: x²≡1 mod 8 has four roots, but 2^ω(8)=2.

The correct standard bounds are:

- If q is odd and gcd(w,q)=1, then ρ_q(w)≤2^ω(q).
- If gcd(w,q)=1 with arbitrary q, then ρ_q(w)≤2^{ω(q)+1}; the extra factor is forced by the four unit roots modulo 2^ν for ν≥3.
- Without coprimality,
  ρ_q(w)≤2^{ω(q)+1}√gcd(w,q)
  is a valid uniform form.
- Alternatively, ρ_q(w)≤2^ω(q) holds for squarefree q.

These follow from prime-power root counts, Hensel lifting, and CRT; Iwaniec–Kowalski §21.3 is the appropriate standard reference.

Worse, a root count in the sampling variable n does not by itself bound the number of pairs (d,d′). The declaration never defines “fixed root datum,” the map from a frequency pair to a root, or the size of its fibers. The parent proposal claims that this manufactures exactly the “map from off-diagonal pairs … to a shared modulus with sub-square multiplicity” missing in the declared large-sieve obstacle, but that map is the hard step. Calling its fibers roots of one congruence does not prove injectivity or bounded multiplicity.

Proving only the valid exponential-sum inequality would therefore be misdirected. Record 010 itself admits “frontier delta: none,” while record 008 leaves the actual G2 risks untouched: W2-G2a found a fixed-negative square-modulus locus of size about 1.42×|E|, and W2-G2b found systematic positive correlation but was inconclusive at H=1. QSCL gives no H=1 cancellation and does not control the fixed-sign locus.

2. THE ASSUMPTIONS MOST LIKELY FALSE

- Statement correctness: “collision multiplicity … bounded by 2^ω(q)” is false as written. Even after correcting the modular root bound, the pair-collision conclusion remains unproved because no pair-to-root fiber theorem is stated.

- Obstacle positioning: record 010 says “none of the analytic obstacles bind,” but its multiplicity clause is precisely an attempted solution to AGENTS.md’s large-sieve non-transfer obstacle. That obstacle therefore binds directly. The declaration also labels q a “small packet conductor,” while the parent only defines q as the reduced denominator of a/d−a′/d′. Nothing in the four files proves that these denominators are small divisors of the relevant torus modulus. AGENTS.md explicitly forbids silently replacing packet-level conductors by full-modulus numerology.

- H=1 relevance: the parent proposal’s self-assessed weakest link is the H=1 bootstrap. The QSCL completion estimate is worse than the trivial bound at H=1, so it provides no leverage on that weakest link.

- Lean feasibility: record 010 allows two agent runs and says the Gauss-sum core must be found or half-built in the first. A read-only inventory of the pinned mathlib v4.24.0 source found no Gauss-sum or Weyl-sum development. The declaration’s statement that “mathlib has Gauss sums for characters” is not supported by the installed source. A proof would have to construct additive characters, orthogonality and completion, shifted quadratic Gauss bounds including even q, interval Fourier-kernel bounds, harmonic estimates, and substantial coercion plumbing. Two runs is not a credible budget.

- Governance of the partial outcome: record 010 permits a Lean statement “stubbed” as an open task. AGENTS.md says the audit rejects sorry, admit, and project-local axiom/constant declarations. Such a stub cannot enter the audited build unless it is only a comment or lives outside the build.

- Log loss: any fixed power (log q)^A with A independent of all parameters is analytically absorbable into q^ε, so it does not itself destroy an ε-level application. But “any log-power loss permitted” is not a frozen theorem until A, its constants, and the parameter ranges are specified. It also does nothing about H=1, and its absorption is irrelevant until the application proves q lies in an acceptable packet-conductor range.

3. TWO OR THREE ALTERNATIVE FIRST-CONTACT EXPERIMENTS

1. Exact collision-map and fiber lemma.

What it is: define every variable in the expanded off-diagonal sum, the reduction (a/d−a′/d′)=b/q, the alleged root datum, and the map from pairs (d,d′,a,a′) to that datum. Prove an actual fiber bound, including prime powers and the 2-part.

Leverage: this directly attacks AGENTS.md’s missing shared-modulus map. It also determines whether Heath-Brown’s quadratic large sieve is applicable at all.

Cheapest kill test: test the proposed fiber theorem symbolically at q=8 and q=9, then enumerate its fibers for one small dyadic D. Any fiber growth not accounted for by the corrected ρ_q(w) bound kills the claimed transfer.

2. H=1 exponent-flow audit.

What it is: start from the parent’s assumed
Σ_{d≤D}M(d)² ≪ D^{1+2σ}
and propagate every D-, N-, and q-power through completion and Heath-Brown’s quadratic large sieve. Produce the explicit map σ↦F(σ).

Leverage: the parent itself says the bootstrap is most likely neutral. Record 008’s W2-G2b verdict is inconclusive, so exact exponent bookkeeping dominates formalizing an upstream lemma that is vacuous at H=1.

Cheapest kill test: if F(σ)≥σ, or if the declared D^{3/2} term survives when H=1, the pointwise route is dead.

3. Exact signed parity-locus isolation.

What it is: isolate square moduli inside the full C−2E signed Gram expression, without triangle-splitting, and identify the exact term that is supposed to cancel their fixed sign.

Leverage: W2-G2a found the locus permanently negative and about 1.42×|E|. That contradicts the parent’s suggestion that “sparsity of squares alone” makes it harmless.

Cheapest kill test: apply the best available sparsity-only bound to the isolated term. If it misses the required scale and no opposite signed term is algebraically paired with it, the parity route fails before any exponential-sum formalization.

4. THE CHEAPEST KILL TEST

Check q=8,w=1 in the frozen companion statement. The four residues 1,3,5,7 all satisfy x²≡1 mod 8, while 2^ω(8)=2. This takes under a minute and disproves QSCL as declared.

After correcting that error, the cheapest feasibility test is a theorem-inventory sheet naming the exact existing mathlib lemmas for additive-character orthogonality, shifted quadratic Gauss bounds, and incomplete completion. The present source inventory found none. Failure to name those lemmas means the “two agent runs” budget is already falsified.

6. WHAT EVIDENCE WOULD REVERSE YOUR OWN VIEW

I would support launch only after all of the following are produced:

- A revised QSCL separating the exponential-sum theorem from the collision theorem, with q≥1, H≥1, N∈ℤ, gcd(b,q)=1, explicit constants, and an even-q branch or explicit √gcd(2b,q) factor.

- Correct multiplicity hypotheses: squarefree q, or unit w with the necessary 2-adic factor, or the general √gcd(w,q) bound.

- A written, checkable pair-to-root map with a proved bounded-fiber statement. Merely counting roots is insufficient.

- A proof that application moduli are genuinely small packet conductors in the sense required by AGENTS.md, rather than potentially large reduced denominators of a/d−a′/d′.

- Exact mathlib theorem names covering most of the completion infrastructure, or a compiled no-sorry prototype of the complete shifted Gauss bound including even q.

- A downstream calculation showing precisely how the chosen log power is absorbed and where QSCL yields a nontrivial gain. For the pointwise route, that calculation must survive H=1.

- A concrete treatment of W2-G2a’s fixed-negative square locus and a quantified criterion resolving W2-G2b’s positive-correlation regime.

Those facts would turn QSCL from a true but strategically inert textbook lemma into a justified first-contact experiment.
